Machine-learning analysis of codon usage bias to distinguish highly and lowly expressed genes in prokaryotes

Paste an E. coli coding sequence (or a FASTA file of many genes). The tool computes codon frequency, RSCU and CAI in your browser, and a Random Forest trained on NCBI E. coli genes predicts whether the gene is highly expressed (HEG) or lowly expressed (LEG).

How Kodon works

Most amino acids can be written by several codons. Genes that a cell makes in large amounts (HEG) lean heavily on the "fast" codons that match its most plentiful tRNAs, while rarely made genes (LEG) use codons more randomly. Kodon learns these habits and reads them back from any gene.

  1. 1

    Read the gene

    The DNA sequence is cut into triplets (codons), in frame from the first base.

  2. 2

    Count codons

    How often each of the 64 codons appears gives 64 codon frequencies.

  3. 3

    Compare synonyms

    RSCU scores each codon against its synonymous siblings: above 1 = preferred. That's 59 more values, 123 features in total.

  4. 4

    Ask the forest

    A Random Forest of 200 decision trees, trained in Python with scikit-learn on random samples of genes and features, looks at the 123 features and votes HEG or LEG.

  5. 5

    Give the answer

    The share of trees voting HEG is P(HEG). 0.5 or more → highly expressed, otherwise lowly expressed.

What it learned from

214 real E. coli K-12 genes from NCBI: 107 known highly expressed (ribosomal proteins, translation factors, chaperones, energy metabolism) and 107 lowly expressed transcriptional regulators.

How well it works

The forest trains on 160 genes and is tested on the other 54 it has never seen. Its score and the codons it relies on most are shown in the model results below.

Built in Python

The whole pipeline is a Jupyter notebook, kodon_model.ipynb: Biopython downloads the genes from NCBI, pandas builds the codon features, scikit-learn trains and evaluates the Random Forest, and matplotlib / seaborn plot the results. The trained trees are exported to this website, which runs them in your browser, so every prediction here comes from the scikit-learn model.

Training data

214 coding sequences of Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655 from NCBI RefSeq (NC_000913.3), labelled by NCBI gene annotation:

  • 107 HEG: ribosomal proteins, translation factors, chaperones, major outer-membrane proteins, glycolysis, TCA and ATP-synthase genes
  • 107 LEG: DNA-binding transcriptional regulators (global regulators such as Fis and H-NS excluded)

Split 75% training / 25% test, stratified by class (scikit-learn train_test_split). Full feature table: kodon_dataset.csv.
