Machine-learning analysis of codon usage bias to distinguish highly and lowly expressed genes in prokaryotes

Paste an E. coli coding sequence (or a FASTA file of many genes). Kodon computes codon frequency, RSCU and CAI in your browser, and a Random Forest trained in Python on NCBI E. coli genes predicts whether the gene is highly expressed (HEG) or lowly expressed (LEG).

How Kodon works

Most amino acids can be written by several codons. Genes that a cell makes in large amounts (HEG) lean heavily on the "fast" codons that match its most plentiful tRNAs, while rarely made genes (LEG) use codons more randomly. Kodon learns these habits and reads them back from any gene.

  1. 01

    Read the gene

    The DNA sequence is cut into triplets (codons), in frame from the first base.

  2. 02

    Count codons

    How often each of the 64 codons appears gives 64 codon frequencies.

  3. 03

    Compare synonyms

    RSCU scores each codon against its synonymous siblings: above 1 = preferred. That's 59 more values, 123 features in total.

  4. 04

    Ask the forest

    A Random Forest of 200 decision trees, trained in Python with scikit-learn, looks at the 123 features and votes HEG or LEG.

  5. 05

    Give the answer

    The share of trees voting HEG is P(HEG). 0.5 or more → highly expressed, otherwise lowly expressed.

What it learned from

208 real E. coli K-12 genes from NCBI: 104 known highly expressed (ribosomal proteins, translation factors, chaperones, energy metabolism) and 104 lowly expressed transcriptional regulators.

Kept apart for testing

Before training, the genes are split in two: the forest learns from 156 genes and is tested on the other 52, which it never sees. Duplicate NCBI records are removed and near-identical gene copies (like tufA / tufB) are kept on the same side, so no test gene leaks into training.

Built in Python

The whole pipeline is a Jupyter notebook, kodon_model.ipynb: Biopython fetches the genes, pandas builds the features, scikit-learn trains and tests the forest, and matplotlib / seaborn plot the results. This site runs the exported trees, so every prediction here is the scikit-learn model's.

Training and test data

208 coding sequences of Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655 from NCBI RefSeq (NC_000913.3), labelled by NCBI gene annotation: 104 HEG (ribosomal proteins, translation factors, chaperones, outer-membrane, glycolysis, TCA and ATP-synthase genes) and 104 LEG (DNA-binding transcriptional regulators).

Split with scikit-learn StratifiedGroupKFold: 156 training genes, 52 test genes, balanced HEG / LEG, with no gene or near-identical copy on both sides. Full table: kodon_dataset.csv.
